This guide assumes you've read [Understanding Laser Types](/guides/understanding-laser-types) — or at least know whether you need diode, CO₂, or fiber.

We organize recommendations by **what you want to make**, not by marketing specs.

> **Transparency:** Maker Atlas does not use affiliate links yet. Recommendations are based on capability, value, and real-world limits — not commissions.

---

## Before you spend anything

### 1. Define your main project (be specific)

Bad: "I want a laser for crafts."
Good: "I want to cut 3mm acrylic keychains and engrave birch plywood coasters."

Your main project determines the laser **type**. Everything else is secondary.

### 2. Budget for essentials

The laser is not the full cost. Plan for:

| Item | Cost | Required? |
|------|------|-----------|
| Laser safety glasses | $15–40 | Yes (open-frame diodes) |
| Air assist | $30–80 | Strongly recommended |
| Honeycomb bed | $40–100 | Recommended for cutting |
| Ventilation hose / fan | $30–150 | Yes for CO₂ and smoke |
| LightBurn license | ~$60–120 | Recommended |
| Fire extinguisher | $20–40 | Yes |

Add **$150–300** to any laser price for a usable setup.

### 3. Ignore these marketing traps

- **Combined wattage** ("60W diode") without optical power listed
- **"Cuts 20mm wood"** without noting multiple passes, char, and hours of time
- **"Engraves everything"** — no desktop laser does everything well
- **Influencer discount codes** as proof of quality

## Recommendations by use case

### "I'm curious — smallest budget"

**Buy:** [Atomstack A5 Pro](/lasers/atomstack-a5-pro) ($200–400) *or* [Ortur Laser Master 3](/lasers/ortur-laser-master-3) ($400–600)

**Honest take:** Atomstack if budget is truly fixed. Ortur if you can stretch $150 more — better long-term value.

**Skip:** CO₂ and fiber — overkill and over budget.

**Expect:** Engraving on wood/leather, light cuts on thin wood. You may upgrade within a year if the hobby sticks.

---

### "Hobby engraver — wood, leather, gifts"

**Buy:** [Ortur Laser Master 3](/lasers/ortur-laser-master-3) 20W ($500–700) or [xTool D1 Pro](/lasers/xtool-d1-pro) 10W/20W ($700–1,000)

**Honest take:** Ortur = best value. xTool = better build, software, and fewer headaches.

**Skip:** Glowforge Aura (subscription + small bed for the price), cheap no-name Amazon lasers with zero community.

**Expect:** Reliable engraving, occasional thin cuts, learning curve on settings.

---

### "I want to cut wood and acrylic for an Etsy shop"

**Buy:** [OMTech 40W CO₂](/lasers/omtech-40w-co2) ($500–900) if you tinker, or [xTool P2](/lasers/xtool-p2) ($4,500+) if you want business-ready out of the box

**Honest take:** The K40-class OMTech can produce sellable products for 5× less money — if you enjoy setup. The P2 is what you buy when downtime costs more than the machine.

**Skip:** High-power diodes as your primary cut tool. They cannot match CO₂ on acrylic.

**Expect:** Real production capability. Ventilation planning is mandatory.

---

### "Metal jewelry, dog tags, knives"

**Buy:** [xTool F1 Ultra](/lasers/xtool-f1-ultra) ($3,500+) for fiber + diode flexibility

**Budget alternative:** [xTool D1 Pro](/lasers/xtool-d1-pro) 20W + marking spray ($700–1,000) — works but slower, less professional on metal

**Skip:** CO₂ (cannot mark bare metal), pure diode without accepting spray workflow.

**Expect:** Small work area on fiber machines. Rotary attachment budget (~$150–300) for rings.

---

### "Crafter who hates technology"

**Buy:** [Glowforge Aura](/lasers/glowforge-aura) ($1,000 + subscription)

**Honest take:** You pay for simplicity. Technical users get better hardware per dollar elsewhere.

**Skip:** Open-frame diodes, K40-class CO₂, anything requiring LightBurn configuration.

**Expect:** Thin material cutting, easy software, ongoing subscription cost.

---

### "Strong diode cuts without CO₂ price"

**Buy:** [Sculpfun S30 Ultra](/lasers/sculpfun-s30-ultra) ($450–750) or [Creality Falcon2 Pro](/lasers/creality-falcon2-pro) ($800–1,100)

**Honest take:** Best diode-class cutting. Still not CO₂ for acrylic production.

**Compare:** [Side-by-side comparison tool](/compare)

---

### "Small desk, small projects only"

**Buy:** [Longer Ray5](/lasers/longer-ray5) ($300–450)

**Honest take:** Engraving-first machine. Not for sign makers.

## Red flags when shopping

1. **No mention of laser type** (diode vs CO₂) — seller may be hiding limitations
2. **Only combined wattage listed** — ask for optical power
3. **No ventilation guidance for CO₂** — irresponsible seller
4. **"Pro" in the name with no work area or precision specs**
5. **Zero community presence** — no Reddit, Facebook group, or LightBurn profiles

## Accessories that actually matter

### Buy early

- **Air assist** — cleaner cuts, less fire risk
- **Laser safety glasses** (OD rating for your wavelength)
- **Ventilation** — even diodes produce harmful smoke

### Buy when needed

- **Rotary roller** — tumblers, mugs, cylindrical items
- **Honeycomb bed** — cutting without flashback burns
- **Enclosure** — smoke control and safety for open-frame machines
- **Camera alignment** — nice for batch work, not essential to start

### Skip at first

- Expensive brand-name plywood (test cheap birch first)
- Multiple power modules before you know your needs
- Premium exhaust filters (window vent works fine to start)

## Software: what to use

| Software | Cost | Best for |
|----------|------|----------|
| **LightBurn** | ~$60–120 | Serious hobby and business — buy this |
| **LaserGRBL** | Free | Budget entry, limited features |
| **xTool Creative Space** | Free | xTool owners, beginner-friendly |
| **Glowforge app** | Subscription | Aura/Pro users only |

**Our advice:** If you buy any open-frame diode or K40-class CO₂, budget for LightBurn. It saves more time than it costs.
